# RAP LIFE — The Roadmap

The plan for turning RAP LIFE into a music-world game people play every day for months, not a game that spikes for a week and fades.
Stages 1–5 (the city) are done. This file lists every stage after that in build order. To continue, say **"continue with stage N"**.

---

## The idea in one line

**A living music nation where every job in the industry is playable, the charts are made by real people, and you build a whole life — school, career, family, legacy — inside it.**

Not everyone becomes a superstar, and that's on purpose. Every career path has its own top: Producer of the Year, Senior Partner, Chief Justice, Union President, the billionaire label owner. Everyone can be great at *something*.

## Design laws (every stage must obey these)

1. **Every session (10–20 min) has a goosebump moment and a decision that matters.** A goosebump moment is something like your song on a stranger's radio, a crowd chanting your hook, a verdict going your way, or passing your bar exam.
2. **Three goals are always visible:** one for today, one for this week, one for this life.
3. **Nothing is pure waiting.** Every timer comes with something to do while it runs.
4. **People cause most events, not scripts:** rivals, clients, voters, judges, fans. That gives endless variety.
5. **Failure makes story.** You fail an exam and retake it. A single flops and the comeback arc starts. You lose a case and appeal.
6. **Status means something.** Charts are limited, fame fades, and talent has a ceiling, so being at the top is real.
7. **Seasons give everyone a fresh shot; legacy is forever.**
8. **Never pay-to-win.** Money buys looks and convenience, never chart positions, votes or verdicts.

## Time

- **Story mode (offline, now):** the clock only runs while you play, with speed controls. Long stretches (school semesters, tours) are played as **time skips with key moments you play**: exams, interviews, the big show.
- **Online world (later stages):** one shared clock for everyone, at **1 real day = 4 game months** (1 game year = 3 real days).
  - A 3-year diploma takes about 9 real days and a 4-year degree about 12 real days.
  - A 30-year career takes about 3 real months, and then the next generation begins.

## The daily / weekly / seasonal / lifetime loops

- **Daily:** missions in the city, your role's work (a session, a client, a class), the open mic, checking your streams, chats.
- **Weekly:** chart day (Saturday), court sessions, exams, paydays, magazine covers.
- **Seasonal:** award nights, Union elections, the season pass, a new district or city.
- **Lifetime:** degrees, career ladder, marriage and kids, retirement, Hall of Fame, your child takes over.

# PHASE A — Great on your own (single player, offline)

## Stage 6 — The first 15 minutes + reasons to come back
- Guided opening: create your character, then in the first 15 minutes you get a first win in your chosen path, a first fan or client, and a first rival.
- **Progressive unlocks:** the deep menus (tax, court, label, markets) stay hidden until your career needs them.
- **City missions:** deliver a beat, a rap battle in the park, a shoot at a landmark, a meeting at Mama's Kitchen.
- **Daily goals** (3 a day) and a weekly goal, with streak rewards that never punish missing a day too hard.
- New districts unlock as your career grows.
- **Analytics hooks** (local first): where players stop, how long sessions last, and what they do first.
- *Done when:* a new player gets their first "wow" moment inside 5 minutes and has a reason to return tomorrow.

## Stage 7 — Choose who you are: the career framework
- At creation you pick a **path**, not just a rapper.
  - **Wave 1:** Artist (rap/sing), Producer, Songwriter, Manager, Lawyer.
- Each path has:
  - **its own career ladder** (e.g. Lawyer: student → junior associate → associate → senior associate → partner → senior partner → judge),
  - **its own Story Director**: chapters, mentors, rivals and moral choices. Examples: Producer — "sell the same beat to two artists?"; Lawyer — "take the shady client?"; Manager — "your artist wants to leave".
  - **its own daily work**, which must be real gameplay, not a button.
- **Difficulty modes:**
  - Easy (connected family, savings)
  - Normal
  - Hard (broke, no network, rougher luck)
  - **Ironman** (one save, no reloads, a badge everyone can see)
- **Talent has a ceiling:** each character is born with hidden potential in each skill. Hard work gets you closer to it; luck and choices do the rest. That's why not everyone becomes a superstar.
- **Switching paths** later in life is possible but costly (back to school, starting at the bottom).
- *Done when:* a Producer and a Lawyer have completely different first hours, and both are fun.

## Stage 8 — School, exams and qualifications
- **Institutions in the city:** a Music Academy, a Law School and a Business School, each with a new building and campus.
- Some paths **require** school (Lawyer, Financial Manager). Others are **optional but help** (Producer, Engineer, Music Director); you can also learn by doing, more slowly.
- **Semesters** run as time skips. You play:
  - **classes as short mini-games:** ear training and chords (music theory is real and educational), contract-spotting (law), budgeting and markets (business)
  - **mid-terms and finals:** a real question bank, a timer, and a pass mark.
- **Fail and you retake**, which costs a semester and money. Fail twice and you're on probation. Cheating can work, or it can get you expelled.
- **GPA matters:** better grades mean better internships and first jobs. Scholarships are available for top students; student loans you must repay.
- **Campus life:** classmates (future colleagues and rivals), a part-time job to pay fees, campus parties, a graduation ceremony.
- **Law is the in-game "Rap Life Code":** a simplified fictional legal system inspired by real ideas (contracts, copyright, sampling, defamation), clearly labelled as fiction.
- *Done when:* a player cheers out loud at passing their bar exam.

## Stage 9 — Every role is a real game (roles wave 2)
- **Producer:** a real beat-maker built on the game's beat engine (step sequencer, drums, chords, 808s). Your beats are actual beats that artists can use.
- **Songwriter:** a lyric game with rhyme help. Sell hooks and verses to artists.
- **Sound Engineer:** a mixing mini-game (levels, EQ, autotune). Better mixes chart better.
- **Music Director / Bandleader:** arrange the live band for shows, cue sections during the set, and lead an artist's tour band.
- **DJ / Radio Host:** run your own show on HOT 101.9. Pick the playlist and talk between songs; your listeners move songs up the charts. Radio power is real power.
- **Video Director:** directed shoots using the city camera: shot lists, locations, takes, editing.
- **Stylist / Fashion Designer:** design clothes for Drip & Ink. Artists wear your designs on stage.
- **Manager:** sign clients, negotiate deals, plan release calendars, handle scandals.
- *Done when:* every listed role has a daily activity a player would choose to do for fun.

## Stage 10 — Justice: lawyers, courts and judges
- Cases come from the world: broken contracts, uncleared samples, diss-track defamation, unpaid feature fees, label disputes.
- **Courtroom gameplay:** gather evidence (messages, contracts and receipts already exist in the game), build arguments from evidence cards, question witnesses, and give a closing statement. A judge rules and a jury of NPCs (later, players) votes.
- **Appeals**, settlements, and a lawyer's reputation and win rate.
- **Judges:** a senior lawyer with enough years and a clean record can apply. A judge rules on cases, is graded on fairness, and can be removed.
- *Done when:* a court case feels like a tense episode, not a form.

## Stage 11 — Institutions, power and elections
- **The Musicians' Union** (inspired by bodies like MUSIGA), with an **elected President**.
  - Elections each game year: candidates campaign, debate, make promises and form alliances. Members vote.
  - **Powers within limits:** the union budget (from dues), festival themes, support funds, award categories, minimum feature fees, strike calls.
  - Scandals, impeachment votes, term limits.
- **The Awards Academy:** senior members vote on awards, and lobbying and campaigning are part of the game.
- **Later:** a city council and mayor (zoning for venues, festival permits, nightlife rules).
- AI candidates and voters until real players fill them (Stage 19).
- *Done when:* "who becomes President this year?" is something players talk about.

## Stage 12 — Life and legacy
- Dating, marriage, friendship and family. Kids grow up with traits from both parents.
- Health, ageing, burnout, rehab and comebacks.
- **Retirement → the next generation:** play as your child, starting with some money, fame and connections.
- **Hall of Fame** across generations and family trees ("the third generation of the Mensah dynasty").
- *Done when:* players care what happens after their character retires.

# PHASE B — Share it with the world

## Stage 13 — Viral clips
- One tap exports a **vertical 9:16 clip with your song's audio** and a watermark/link: open mic sets, video shoots, award wins, courtroom victories.
- Song cards, chart cards and "I passed the bar" cards, shareable to TikTok, Reels, Status and X.
- Friend invites with rewards for both players.

## Stage 14 — Ready for millions of phones
- Accounts, cloud saves and multiple devices (the server already exists and gets expanded).
- **App release:** an installable web app first, then Android and iOS store versions.
- **Low-end Android first:** quality settings, data-light, works offline in story mode.
- A real analytics dashboard: retention, session length, drop-off points.
- **Store readiness:** age rating, privacy, and **gambling features (Casino, Sportsbook) behind 18+ or removed** in the store build, because they block mass-market launch.

## Stage 15 — Real music from real players
- Record your verse (the booth exists), then **publish** the song to the global in-game Streams.
- Real global charts, follows, likes, comments, and features with other players.
- **Producers' beats + artists' vocals = real collaborations** between players.
- Safety: lyric and audio moderation, copyright checks, reporting, age gates.

# PHASE C — A living world together

## Stage 16 — The online world
- A shared world clock (see Time) with server-controlled money, charts and contracts.
- See other players in the city in real time, in shared "city instances" that scale up as more people play.
- Chat with safety tools (filters, block, report, chat that keeps strangers out for young players).

## Stage 17 — Players fill the jobs
- Every role in the world is played by a **player when one exists, or an AI character when none does**, so the world feels full on launch day and gets richer as people join.
- **Player contracts enforced by the game:** label deals, management percentages, beat leases, legal retainers, band hire.
- A job board: an artist posts "need a music director for my tour", and players apply.

## Stage 18 — Live events
- **Concerts with real audiences:** the artist performs (the rhythm set), the music director leads the band, and the promoter sells tickets.
- **Battles:** rap battles, producer beat-offs, DJ clashes, judged by crowd votes.
- **Award nights live:** red carpet in the city, speeches, a broadcast.

## Stage 19 — Real elections, real courts
- Union President elected by players (one person, one vote, protected against bots).
- Player lawyers, player judges and player juries on real disputes between players.

## Stage 20 — Own the city
- Player-owned businesses: studios, venues, restaurants (become Mama's Kitchen's rival), fashion stores, radio stations, labels.
- Property, rent, staff (players or AI), and competition between businesses.

---

# PHASE D — Forever

## Stage 21 — Creator marketplace
- Sell beats, clothes, stage designs and venue layouts, with a revenue share for creators.

## Stage 22 — Seasons
- Themed seasons ("Afrobeats Summer", "Drill Winter", "Highlife Heritage"), a season pass (cosmetics only), and ranked charts that reset each season while the Hall of Fame stays.

## Stage 23 — New cities
- Lagos, London, Atlanta and Johannesburg, each with local genres, slang, venues and institutions.
- Tours, travel, and international charts.

## Stage 24 — Talent show seasons
- "RAP LIFE Idol": a seasonal competition across every role (best artist, producer, DJ, lawyer, director), with live finals streamed.

## Stage 25 — The real-world bridge
- Partner labels scout the top in-game artists and producers, playlist placements, brand stages in the city.

---

## Running alongside every stage
- **Performance on cheap phones** gets checked at every stage.
- **Safety:** moderation, reporting, age-appropriate defaults, no gambling for minors.
- **Fairness:** server-side checks once things go online; no pay-to-win.
- **AI cost control:** on-device or cached AI for characters, with the bring-your-own-key option as an extra rather than a requirement.
- **Name check:** once you can be a lawyer, judge or producer, "RAP LIFE" may feel narrow. Consider a broader name (or "RAP LIFE: Music Nation") before the store launch.
