# AKWAABA TONIGHT — the story bible

> *One song every person in the country knows. One voice nobody can name.*

This is the spine of RAP LIFE. It is not a quest list. It is a **living story that the player walks into from four different seats** — a young rapper, a young producer, a young manager, a young singer-songwriter — and that remembers everything they do.

Everything is **fictional**. No real artists, labels or people.

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

Twenty-five years ago a song called **"Akwaaba Tonight"** made a record label, a legend and a nation. The singer on it was credited as *Naa Kai* — a name invented for the sleeve. The voice was never Naa Kai's. The woman who really sang it now cooks jollof at **Mama's Kitchen**, the place where the whole neighbourhood eats, argues and freestyles. She has never told anyone. Her son is the hottest young rapper on the block. The label that owns the song is about to throw its **25th-anniversary gala**, and every person you've come to love in this city is about to be pulled into the same room — each holding one piece of the truth, each with a reason to bury it.

You will find the truth. **Whether you tell it, to whom, when, and what it costs — is the game.**

## 2. What the story is *about*

- **Credit.** Who gets paid, who gets named, who gets remembered. (This is the real music industry: splits, ghostwriters, uncredited producers, stolen hooks.)
- **Loyalty vs. ambition.** Every door that opens for you closes behind someone you love.
- **Silence.** Some characters kept quiet to survive. Is that betrayal, or love?
- **Becoming.** Nobody is only a hero or a villain. Even the antagonist has a reason you can understand and a line you can watch her cross.

Tone: warm, funny, street-smart, with real stakes. Food, family, slang, rain on tin roofs, studios at 3 a.m. Think *a Spike Lee block party that turns into a courtroom drama*.

## 3. Rules that make it feel alive

1. **Missions arrive like life, not like a menu.** A phone call at 2 a.m. A text that says only *"You up?"* A person standing outside the studio. A voice note. A letter under the door. A radio host saying your name. *Never* a floating "Mission Available" card.
2. **You can ignore anything.** Ignored calls and unanswered texts are *choices*. The story moves on without you, and the people remember. ("I called you that night.")
3. **No fail screen.** Failing a task changes the story. A flopped showcase puts you on the *underground road*; a lost client puts you on the *rebuild road*. There is always a way forward, and some of the best scenes are on the "wrong" road.
4. **Gameplay is inside the story.** The booth, the beat maker, the mix desk, the shoot and the editor are *how the scenes are resolved*. "Static needs a hook before sunrise" is a recording session whose **take score decides which scene plays next** — not a checkbox.
5. **Characters remember, specifically.** Every important choice writes a *memory flag*. Later dialogue lines are conditional on them. Characters quote you back to yourself, forgive you, or don't.
6. **Three kinds of reputation, not one number.** **Integrity** (do you keep your word?), **Hustle** (do you get things done, however?), **Heart** (do you take care of people?). Different doors open for different people.
7. **Time is real.** Some scenes only exist in a window ("Mama's Kitchen, Friday night"). Some consequences land weeks later, when you've forgotten the choice.
8. **Nobody is safe, nobody is doomed.** Characters can be lost, lose their way, or come back. The same character can end three different ways depending on you.

## 4. The world

The story runs in **your home city** (the default flavour is Accra — Jamestown, Osu, Labadi, Kokomlemle — but every place name is a token, so it reads as *your* city).

**Key places** (all already in the city, and each becomes a story stage):
- **Mama's Kitchen** — the heart. Friday-night cypher, the family table, where the truth hides in plain sight.
- **Studio 9** — Ankh's studio. Reel 9 sits in the tape room and nobody is allowed to touch it.
- **The Stage** — open mic, where careers start and end in four minutes.
- **First Capital** (the bank) — where the money, the loans and the royalty statements live.
- **Gold Coast Sound (GCS)** — the label tower on the hill. Glass, cold air, good coffee.
- **The Midnight Cypher** — the radio show nobody in the city misses.

## 5. The cast

Each character has a **want** (what they chase), a **need** (what they actually lack), a **secret**, and **three possible endings** shaped by you.

### Mama Esi Armah (58) — *the voice*
Owner of Mama's Kitchen. Feeds half the block on credit. Hums while she cooks and stops mid-line when someone listens.
- **Want:** to keep the kitchen open, and her son safe.
- **Need:** to be heard — once — as herself.
- **Secret:** she is the voice on "Akwaaba Tonight". In 1999 she was 33, a backing singer with a baby. She signed a paper she couldn't afford a lawyer to read. She walked away from music and has *never sung in front of anyone since*.
- **Endings:** she sings again at the gala · she is credited and compensated but never sings again · she is exposed against her will and withdraws · (low Heart + early reveal) she suffers a stroke in the kitchen and the story continues without her voice — *loss*.

### Nii "Static" Armah (23) — *the friend and the rival*
Rapper. Grew up in the flat above the kitchen. If you're a rapper he is your childhood duo-partner ("Two Rooms"); in other paths he is the artist you cross paths with. Fast, funny, wildly talented, secretly terrified of being ordinary.
- **Want:** to be the biggest name in the country.
- **Need:** to know where he comes from.
- **Secret:** he doesn't know his mother is the voice. He thinks *Naa Kai* is a ghost his mother is oddly sad about.
- **Endings:** brothers-in-arms · bitter rivals · he walks away from music and goes abroad · (betrayed early *and* ignored) he spirals — a night in a cell, a record he tears up, a long road back.

### Kofi "Ankh" Danso (61) — *the legend who lost everything*
Producer of "Akwaaba Tonight". Owns Studio 9 on a loan he can't service. Drinks less than he used to, but not as little as he says.
- **Want:** to save the studio.
- **Need:** forgiveness — his own.
- **Secret:** he signed the masters away to keep the studio open in 1999. He knew whose voice it was. He took the credit and the shame.
- **Endings:** he finishes the *Reel 9 mixtape* and gets his name back · he dies at the console after finishing it (bittersweet, *loss*) · he sells the studio to GCS and disappears.

### Efua Mensah-Quartey (54) — *the CEO*
Founder of Gold Coast Sound. Elegant, funny, terrifying. Genuinely loves music. Genuinely believes the industry would have died without her.
- **Want:** a spotless 25th anniversary and a succession plan.
- **Need:** absolution.
- **Secret:** in 1999 the label was weeks from collapse. She took the masters from Ankh, wrote Esi out, and for 25 years has quietly paid Esi through a "catering contract" so the kitchen never shut. **She is part villain, part guardian**, and the game never lets you decide which in advance.
- **Endings:** destroyed by exposure · quietly settles and survives · *confesses herself* on gala night (only if you've earned her trust) · or **you** end up running her label.

### Mimi Owusu-Banson (34) — *the sharp manager*
GCS's rising A&R / manager. Funny, relentless, a little lonely. Mentor and rival to the manager path.
- **Want:** a seat at the table. **Need:** one person who isn't a client or a boss.
- **Endings:** partner · poacher · protégée who surpasses you.

### Adwoa Sarpong (27) — *the singer*
Afro-fusion singer on the rise. Ambitious, warm, careful with her heart. A romance option and a client/collaborator.
- **Want:** a career on her terms. **Need:** someone who doesn't want a piece of it.
- **Secret:** she's been quietly offered a GCS deal that comes with a clause she hasn't shown anyone.

### Selasi Tetteh (31) — *the filmmaker*
Music-video director with one small film he can't afford to finish. Gentle, stubborn, visual. A romance option and the creative partner for videos.
- **Want:** to make the film. **Need:** to believe he's allowed to.

### Kwabena "Radio K" Asante (45) — *the gatekeeper*
Host of **The Midnight Cypher**. Hears everything first. Can make you with one play or end you with silence. Not corrupt — *tired*.
- Remembers whether you showed up on time, and whether you talked about people behind their backs.

### "Big Moses" Appiah (48) — *the money*
Promoter and fixer. Pays cash, asks small favours, never forgets. He funded the first Akwaaba tour in 1999 — he knows more than he says.
- **Role:** temptation. Every path meets him; every path pays him in a different currency.

### Afia Boakye (29) — *the lawyer*
Idealistic music lawyer, three years out of law school, sharing an office with a dentist. She is the way the truth becomes *official*. She can't do it without you, and she needs you to be brave.

### Fiifi (16) — *the kid who watches*
Lives on the block. Carries your gear. Wants to be you. **Everything you do, he sees.** In the late game he is the measure of what you became: the next voice, or a cautionary tale.

### Lady Zee (30) — *the star*
GCS's biggest pop name, who just scored a hit with an "Akwaaba Tonight" remake. Not the villain — she's another person who found out the song is bigger than the people in it.

## 6. The spine — what really happened (the Ledger)

**1999.** Studio 9, a hot night. **Ankh** produces. **Efua**, then a young A&R with a loan from **Big Moses** to finish the record, needs a hit or the label dies. **Esi**, 33, backing singer, sings the hook and then the whole song in one take. It's a miracle. The label realises the whole country will love it — and that an unknown backing vocalist with a baby and no lawyer is a *liability*. A "star name" is cheaper to invent than a woman is to pay. **Naa Kai** is born on the sleeve. Esi signs. Ankh, with the studio in debt, signs the masters over. Reel 9 — the raw multitrack, her voice with nothing on it — goes into the tape room and Ankh swears nobody will ever touch it.

**Today.** The song is the country's unofficial anthem. GCS is about to license it to a streaming giant for a figure that could save or sink the label. The 25th-anniversary gala is in the diary. Lady Zee's remake is climbing the charts. Esi still gets her "catering contract" cheque every month. **Static**, rising fast, is being groomed by GCS — and has started humming his mother's hook in his verses without knowing why.

### The three truths (each is a separate reveal)
1. **The voice** — *Esi is the singer.*
2. **The paper** — *how Ankh and Efua erased her and why.*
3. **The money** — *the catering contract: Efua has been paying her all along. The kitchen is alive because of the people who wronged her.*

Most players find truth 1 first. **Truth 3 is the one that breaks people's moral compass.**

### How each seat finds it
- **Rapper:** Static freestyles a hook he can't place. You hear Mama Esi hum it, same pitch, same grace-note. Then you hear Reel 9.
- **Producer:** a sample-clearance job for the *Akwaaba* remake leads you to the split sheet — one name missing. You isolate the vocal stem from Reel 9. You recognise the phrasing from the kitchen.
- **Manager:** you audit a client's GCS statements and find the monthly payments to *Mama's Kitchen Catering Ltd — Naa Kai advance recoup*.
- **Singer-songwriter:** GCS asks you to *sing* "Akwaaba Tonight" at the gala. The key, the ad-libs, the breath placement — they aren't Naa Kai's. You walk into the kitchen one afternoon and hear it, as it was always meant to be sung, under the sound of a pot lid.

## 7. The four core seats

Each path has **its own first hours, its own daily work, its own moral dilemmas, and its own ending** — but all four share the cast and the Ledger, so *the same events look different from each seat*.

### A. THE RAPPER — "Two Rooms"
*You and Static grew up one flat apart. He got the speakers; you got the notebook.*

**Act I — Ground Floor (days 1–60)**
1. **Friday Cypher.** Mama's Kitchen on cypher night. Static pulls you into a circle. Mama hums over the pots, stops when you look. *First scene teaches choices: join the circle, stay at the back, or help Mama carry plates.* (Helping Mama = first Heart + the first thing she remembers.)
2. **Say Something You've Never Said.** Ankh lets you into Studio 9 for one take. He doesn't want a hit — he wants a truth. The take score decides how he treats you: *respect*, *polite*, or *"come back when you've lived"*. Failing leads to Ankh's *"the long way"* road: sweeping, listening, learning — a slower, richer bond.
3. **The Hook We Can't Clear.** Static has a beat built on a vocal loop he found. It's the hook. He wants your verse. You must **record by sunrise**. Quality decides whether Radio K hears it.
4. **The Midnight Cypher.** Radio K invites you both. On air you can: **cut Static down** (the crowd loves it, it hurts him), **lift him up** (he's seen), or **go quiet** (strange, magnetic). *The first betrayal-or-loyalty fork.*
5. **Big Moses's Envelope.** After the show a man hands you cash for "a private party." The party is fine. It's what you owe afterwards that isn't.

**Act II — The Price (days 60–220)**
- Static signs with GCS. His first single is *"Akwaaba (Remix)"*. You're offered a place on the label's "street team" — a smaller deal, a gilded cage.
- Mama's health. Ankh's debt. Fiifi's loyalty. Adwoa or Selasi (romance). **The midpoint betrayal:** Static's team asks you for a favour that would bury you (or *you* are offered a leak that would bury him).
- You find Reel 9.

**Act III — The Night of Akwaaba (days 220+)**
The gala. Every thread converges. *You* are the one who can walk onstage with the truth — or not.

**Rapper endings:** Voice of the People (the credit restored, your name tied to it) · Rival Kings (Static and you split the city) · The Quiet Legend (you walk away from the stage, keeping the truth) · Loser's Redemption (you lost everything, Esi sings, and you're proud).

### B. THE PRODUCER — "Reel 9"
*Your beats sell for $20 on the internet. Ankh needs a pair of hands. Nobody tells you what's in the tape room.*

**Act I**
1. **The Floor Sweeper.** First scene is *mopping Studio 9 at 4 a.m.* Ankh lets you press one key. The sound of that key is your first lesson.
2. **The Beat Sold Twice.** You've sold the same beat to Static *and* a new singer (Adwoa). Neither knows. One of them finds out *first*. What you say next — and when — is Integrity vs. Hustle, played out in a phone call.
3. **The Clearance.** GCS asks you to clear a sample for Lady Zee's remake. The paper trail has a name missing.
4. **Reel 9.** Ankh catches you in the tape room. *Do you lie? Confess? Offer to help?* His response is a monologue that doesn't repeat.
5. **The Mix War.** A rival engineer wants credit for your work.

**Act II** — you become the sound of the city. Your credit lines are fought over. You discover the vocal stem. You must decide *who gets the first listen*.
**Producer endings:** The Name on the Sleeve (production credit & publishing share restored to all) · The Quiet Craftsman (you remix the truth into a sound only a few understand) · The Hired Hand (you cash in and become GCS's in-house genius) · The Burned Bridge.

### C. THE MANAGER — "Hands"
*You don't make the music. You make the people who do. You hold their contracts and their secrets, and you sleep badly.*

**Act I**
1. **The First Client.** You've booked shows for friends for free. Adwoa gives you a shot on one condition: *"don't treat me like a product."*
2. **Mimi's Offer.** Mimi sees you coming. She offers a mentorship that comes with a hook.
3. **The Shady Client.** Big Moses brings a *very* profitable client who has done *something*.
4. **The Statement.** You request GCS's royalty statements to renegotiate a deal and find a line that makes no sense: monthly payments to a catering company.
5. **Your Artist Wants to Leave.** Adwoa gets a GCS offer with a clause she hid. *Fight, release, or negotiate.*

**Act II** — you now hold *three* clients with *conflicting* interests (Adwoa, Static, a new kid). You can only win for two. **Manager endings:** The Trusted Hand (a small roster that loves you) · The Empire (you own everyone; nobody trusts you) · The Fixer (you sell one client to save another) · The Reckoning (you do the right thing and lose the company).

### D. THE SINGER-SONGWRITER — "The Key"
*You write for others and sing for no one. Your best hook is on someone else's record.*

**Act I**
1. **The Hook in Someone Else's Mouth.** You discover your hook on a GCS single credited to another writer. You can **sue, confront, steal it back, or let it go.**
2. **The Studio Session.** Selasi asks you to score a short film for free; you're broke.
3. **The Gala Invitation.** GCS invites you to sing "Akwaaba Tonight" at the gala. A *huge* break. *It's the first time you hear the original in full.*
4. **The Kitchen.** You walk into Mama's Kitchen and hear the song as it should be sung. **The recognition scene.**
5. **The Voice.** A bad month, a throat infection. *Rest or push?* Your choice determines whether you can perform on the night.

**Songwriter endings:** The Duet (you and Esi sing it together) · The Understudy (you sing it for her, in her honour) · The Whistleblower (you refuse to sing and tell the truth) · The Star (you sing as Naa Kai, and live with it).

## 8. The Night of Akwaaba (the shared finale)

The 25th-anniversary gala at the **Independence Arch Arena**. Efua opens with a speech. Lady Zee sings the remix. A hush. Then the lights fall for "the original, restored by a very special voice".

**What can happen depends on you.** There are three questions and every player answers them differently:
1. **Who walks onstage?** (Esi, you, Lady Zee, Static, no one.)
2. **Who speaks first?** (Efua confesses, you reveal, Ankh finally talks, Static finds out live.)
3. **What does the room do?** (Rises, boos, goes silent, records everything.)

Beyond the finale, the world keeps going. The consequences are **permanent** — the label's status, who owns the song, whether Mama's Kitchen is a landmark or a ruin, what Fiifi does — and a second playthrough from another seat shows the same night from a different angle.

## 9. How choices are remembered

- **Bonds** (−100..100) per character: trust, not just liking.
- **Integrity / Hustle / Heart**: three reputation axes.
- **Flags**: facts ("Ankh knows you were in the tape room", "Static found out from the radio", "Esi heard you ignore her call").
- **A memory line** written to the Journal for every important decision, so the player can read their own story back.
- **A consequence queue**: choices schedule future scenes (days or weeks later), so *the call you ignored in March is the call that comes back in June*.
- **Conditional dialogue**: the same scene reads differently if you were kind, cruel, absent, or honest.

## 10. Delivery channels (how missions find you)

| Channel | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Call** | Your phone rings anywhere. Answer, decline, or let it ring out. |
| **Text** | A message thread with real replies. Some expire if you leave them on read. |
| **Encounter** | Someone is waiting somewhere, and you meet them when you go there. |
| **Letter / mail** | A paper in your mailbox. |
| **World** | A radio line, a headline, a poster, a rumour on the street. |
| **Scene** | A moment that simply *begins* when you walk into a place at the right time. |

## 11. Variety, not repetition

No scene is "release a song". A song is how you *answer* something: a verse to repair a friendship, a hook to save a studio, a beat to lie convincingly, a mix to tell the truth. The booth, the beat maker, the mixing desk, the shoot and Director's Cut are the **instruments of the story**, not the point of it.

## 12. The Personal Layer (people who knew you before the music)

The mystery is the *plot*. This layer is the *life*. It runs under every seat.

### 12.1 KJ — the childhood friend and the shortcut (5 chapters, all seats)
KJ grew up two streets from you. He is funny, loyal, and always one idea away from trouble. The arc is a slow question: **what do you owe the person who knew you when you had nothing?**

1. **Broke** (encounter, city): KJ needs money, now. Take his $2,500 "investment", part-fund him with a small loan, or refuse.
2. **The Bag** (call): he asks you to hold a bag for a night. Take it, peek, ask, or stay out.
3. **The Favour** (call): he needs an alibi. Peeking pays off here: the bag held 1999 ticket stubs stamped *M. APPIAH PRODUCTIONS*. KJ is tangled with Big Moses without knowing what he holds. This is where the mystery touches your own street.
4. **What You Owe** (encounter, kitchen): the debt comes due. Pay, negotiate, help, betray him, or refuse.
5. **Where He Lands** (encounter, shows): he ends **inside** your circle, **away** from it, **cut off**, or as a **partner**. Every ending is livable; none is free.

KJ remembers every answer. Money you lent, calls you ignored, what you said about him when he wasn't there.

### 12.2 Family — Mum, and the person you were at home
Small, quiet scenes triggered by fame, days and money, not by missions: Mum's hobby, the poster she hung of you, the house, a low night, the day she hears you on the radio. They carry **no reward**. They exist so success costs something and feels like something. Mum also hears, in time, whichever of the Ledger's truths reaches the radio first.

### 12.3 The Whispers (the mystery arriving in the open world)
Delivered without a mission card, and **ignorable**:
- A billboard that is wrong: a face painted over with a date.
- The radio: Ankh walks past and turns it off mid-song.
- **UNKNOWN NUMBER**: "Stop asking about that song."
- If you ignore all of it, the world **moves on** without you. Static and Efua close a door you could have opened. You can play a full career and never solve it, and that is a real ending, not a failure.

### 12.4 Humor
Not every scene is heavy. KJ's borrowed car. A reviewer who writes "you rhymed money with money". The industry is funny. The sad scenes only work because the funny ones are true.

### 12.5 No-reward emotional moments
Some scenes give nothing: no money, no bond, no flag. A phone call to Mum. A quiet bus ride. Nothing pops up. They are there so the player stops optimising for a moment.

## 13. Seats added: Director and DJ

**Director — "Frame".** You see what the camera sees. A second-unit job turns into a set that keeps going wrong, and your *footage* reveals what nobody else noticed: the Akwaaba video shows the star lip-syncing "Naa Kai", and a woman singing from the wings. Your truth is **visual**: you can prove things no one else can, but you cannot say what they mean.

**DJ — "The Room".** You read crowds. A club call becomes a set, and a crate of old tape contains the archive reel where the announcer says "Esi, on the hook!" Your truth is **aural and historical**: you know the song that never charted and who was in the room.

### Information asymmetry (each seat holds a different piece)
| Seat | You uniquely know | You can't know |
|---|---|---|
| Rapper | The verse was changed | Who signed it away |
| Producer | The tape, the stems | The money |
| Manager | The contract | The voice |
| Singer | The key and the melody | The paper trail |
| Director | The footage | The studio session |
| DJ | The room, the reel | The contract |

## 14. Careers connect (and hybrids)
- Your seat is not a cage. A Rapper who starts producing his own beats picks up the Producer's scenes. A Manager who films a video gets the Director's.
- **Connected careers**: every seat has at least one scene where another seat's person appears as *your* contact (the Producer who made your beat, the Manager who ran your show).
- **Hybrids** are the long term goal: a rapper-producer, a singer-manager. The engine keeps one **bond and flag set**, so switching seats carries your history with it.

## 15. What kind of person are you becoming (endings)
Endings are not good/bad. They are portraits, written from your three reputation axes and your five or six biggest choices:
- **The Keeper**: high integrity; you carried the truth even when it cost you.
- **The Closer**: high hustle; you won, and the people behind you know how.
- **The Heart**: high heart; you rebuilt what the label broke.
- **The Ghost**: you ignored the mystery; the story walked on without you.
- **The Mirror**: your reputation is mixed; both sides tell different stories of you.

**No game over.** Broke, cancelled, or ignored, the world keeps going. A bad year is a chapter.

## 16. The gala is a convergence, not the end
Everything lands on the Night of Akwaaba: KJ in the crowd, Mum on the phone, the bag's tickets in somebody's hand. After it, the game continues: the *after-gala* months open new arcs (the label rebuilt, the song relicensed, Mama's Kitchen reopened or sold) and a **new-game-plus** lets you play the same night from another seat. The world remembers across playthroughs: a character you betrayed once greets you differently the second time.
