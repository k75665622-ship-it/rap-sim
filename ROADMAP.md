# RAP LIFE — The Roadmap

The plan for turning RAP LIFE into a music-world game people play every day for months, not a game that spikes for a week and fades.
Stages 1–5 (the city) are done. This file lists everything after that. To continue, say **"continue with M1"** (or any stage).

---

## The idea in one line

**A living music nation where every job in the industry is playable, the charts are made by real people, and you build a whole life — school, career, family, legacy — inside it.**

Not everyone becomes a superstar, and that's on purpose. Every career path has its own top: Producer of the Year, Senior Partner, Chief Justice, Union President, the billionaire label owner. Everyone can be great at *something*.

## Design laws (every stage must obey these)

1. **Every session (10–20 min) has a goosebump moment and a decision that matters.** A goosebump moment is something like your song on a stranger's radio, a crowd chanting your hook, a verdict going your way, or passing your bar exam.
2. **Three goals are always visible:** one for today, one for this week, one for this life.
3. **Nothing is pure waiting.** Every timer comes with something to do while it runs.
4. **People cause most events, not scripts:** rivals, clients, voters, judges, fans. That gives endless variety.
5. **Failure makes story.** You fail an exam and retake it. A single flops and the comeback arc starts. You lose a case and appeal.
6. **Status means something.** Charts are limited, fame fades, and talent has a ceiling, so being at the top is real.
7. **Seasons give everyone a fresh shot; legacy is forever.**
8. **Never pay-to-win.** Money buys looks and convenience, never chart positions, votes or verdicts.

## Time

- **Story mode (offline, now):** the clock only runs while you play, with speed controls. Long stretches (school semesters, tours) are played as **time skips with key moments you play**: exams, interviews, the big show.
- **Online world (later stages):** one shared clock for everyone, at **1 real day = 4 game months** (1 game year = 3 real days).
  - A 3-year diploma takes about 9 real days and a 4-year degree about 12 real days.
  - A 30-year career takes about 3 real months, and then the next generation begins.

## The daily / weekly / seasonal / lifetime loops

- **Daily:** missions in the city, your role's work (a session, a client, a class), the open mic, checking your streams, chats.
- **Weekly:** chart day (Saturday), court sessions, exams, paydays, magazine covers.
- **Seasonal:** award nights, Union elections, the season pass, a new district or city.
- **Lifetime:** degrees, career ladder, marriage and kids, retirement, Hall of Fame, your child takes over.

# TRACK M — The music tools (real apps inside the game)

Two separate apps, like real life:
- **VOX** — the vocal booth, for artists. Easy and fun, in the style of Voloco.
- **STUDIO PRO** — the professional workstation, for producers and engineers. Deep and hard, in the style of FL Studio, Ableton or Adobe Audition.

The better you are at the hard tool, the better the music sounds. That gives every role a real skill to master.

**How a song gets made (like real life):**
1. The artist records in VOX.
2. If you're multi-talented, you mix it yourself. If not, you **send it to a producer or engineer**.
3. They work on it in STUDIO PRO and send it back with notes: "tightened your timing, slap delay on the hook, a little more air on the vocal".
4. You ask for revisions or approve it, then release.

Producers and engineers are played by **expert AI characters** until real players take those jobs. AI experts range from cheap beginners to legends; the better they are, the more they cost and the longer they're booked up.

## M2 — Expert AI engineers and producers, and the "send it to a pro" workflow
**Status: M2 done** — the send-to-mix flow already existed; M2 added named tiers (Student/Local/Pro/Legend) with price, days and rounds included shown up front, a specialty style per engineer, late deliveries for cheaper ones, honest feedback when your take is the problem, and de-esser/noise cleanup in their mixes. - **They really process your audio:** a mixing chain that runs on the device, tuned by their skill. Better engineers use more precise pitch correction, de-essing, automation, wider stereo and louder masters.
- The mix comes back with written notes and a before/after comparison. You can request revisions.
- Relationships, rates, deadlines and drama: late delivery, "this needs a better take", disputes.
- Built so a **real player** can later take the same job and use the same tools (M3).

- **Project files** you can send to someone else and get back.
- Hard by design: a bad mix sounds bad, and a great mix charts better.

## M7 — The creator economy (real money, after the online stages)
- **Two kinds of money:**
  - **Coins:** the in-game currency, earned by everyone, never cashed out.
  - **A real-money Creator Market**, for **verified adults only**.
- **Earning money:**
  - **Selling services:** beats, mixing, mastering, verses and hooks, video edits, clothing designs, legal work.
  - **Basic tools are free;** experts set their own prices; the game takes a small fee per sale.
- **How artists earn real money:**
  - a **creator pool**: a share of game revenue (ads and subscriptions) paid out by real listens of published songs, with protection against fake streams
  - **tips and gifts** during live in-game concerts, and **paid tickets** for headline shows
  - **fan club subscriptions** (monthly support, exclusive songs and behind-the-scenes content)
  - **brand deals:** a real chop bar or phone brand pays an artist to promote them in the game (billboards, a mention in a song, a sponsored show), always labelled as an ad
  - **selling verses and features** to other artists, and **custom songs and shout-outs** for fans (birthday songs)
  - **merch:** in-game outfits made with designers, with the sales split
  - **real-world bridge:** sending songs to real streaming platforms, real gigs booked through partner promoters
- **How the money moves (the safe design):**
  - **The game never holds players' money.** No real-money wallets or balances, because holding other people's funds needs a Bank of Ghana payment licence (Act 987).
  - **Payment goes through a licensed provider's split payments** (e.g. Paystack subaccounts). The buyer pays, the seller's share goes straight to their MoMo or bank account, and the game keeps its fee.
  - **Sellers onboard once:** 18+, ID, and a MoMo or bank account.
  - **Preview before you pay** for services: the engineer delivers a tagged/watermarked mix, and the buyer pays to unlock the clean files. That protects both sides without the game holding money in escrow.
  - **Beats and other ready-made items:** pay, then download instantly.
  - **Licence types:** lease vs exclusive, written into every sale.
  - Refunds and disputes go through support and the provider. In-game courts are for coin disputes only.
  - **Start with coins first** (AI and players trading services for in-game coins) to prove people want it, then switch on real money for verified sellers.
  - **Web first.** App store versions may have to follow store billing rules for digital items; check these before launching in the apps.
- **Before any cash payout goes live:**
  - a payment provider that handles payouts and identity checks
  - age verification
  - tax and anti-fraud rules
  - clear rules on who owns each song or beat (the creator does; the game gets a licence to play it)
  - **advice from a lawyer in Ghana** on payout and marketplace rules
- **What the game must avoid:** a tradeable token or crypto-style coin, and cash prizes with entry fees (those can count as gambling).

## Stage 8 — School, exams and qualifications
- **Institutions in the city:** a Music Academy, a Law School and a Business School, each with a new building and campus.
- Some paths **require** school (Lawyer, Financial Manager). Others are **optional but help** (Producer, Engineer, Music Director); you can also learn by doing, more slowly.
- **Semesters** run as time skips. You play:
  - **classes as short mini-games:** ear training and chords (music theory is real and educational), contract-spotting (law), budgeting and markets (business)
  - **mid-terms and finals:** a real question bank, a timer, and a pass mark.
- **Fail and you retake**, which costs a semester and money. Fail twice and you're on probation. Cheating can work, or it can get you expelled.
- **GPA matters:** better grades mean better internships and first jobs. Scholarships are available for top students; student loans you must repay.
- **Campus life:** classmates (future colleagues and rivals), a part-time job to pay fees, campus parties, a graduation ceremony.
- **Law is the in-game "Rap Life Code":** a simplified fictional legal system inspired by real ideas (contracts, copyright, sampling, defamation), clearly labelled as fiction.
- *Done when:* a player cheers out loud at passing their bar exam.

## Stage 9 — Every role is a real game (roles wave 2)
- **Producer:** a real beat-maker built on the game's beat engine (step sequencer, drums, chords, 808s). Your beats are actual beats that artists can use.
- **Songwriter:** a lyric game with rhyme help. Sell hooks and verses to artists.
- **Sound Engineer:** a mixing mini-game (levels, EQ, autotune). Better mixes chart better.
- **Music Director / Bandleader:** arrange the live band for shows, cue sections during the set, and lead an artist's tour band.
- **DJ / Radio Host:** run your own show on HOT 101.9. Pick the playlist and talk between songs; your listeners move songs up the charts. Radio power is real power.
- **Video Director:** directed shoots using the city camera: shot lists, locations, takes, editing.
- **Stylist / Fashion Designer:** design clothes for Drip & Ink. Artists wear your designs on stage.
- **Manager:** sign clients, negotiate deals, plan release calendars, handle scandals.
- *Done when:* every listed role has a daily activity a player would choose to do for fun.

## Stage 10 — Justice: lawyers, courts and judges
- Cases come from the world: broken contracts, uncleared samples, diss-track defamation, unpaid feature fees, label disputes.
- **Courtroom gameplay:** gather evidence (messages, contracts and receipts already exist in the game), build arguments from evidence cards, question witnesses, and give a closing statement. A judge rules and a jury of NPCs (later, players) votes.
- **Appeals**, settlements, and a lawyer's reputation and win rate.
- **Judges:** a senior lawyer with enough years and a clean record can apply. A judge rules on cases, is graded on fairness, and can be removed.
- *Done when:* a court case feels like a tense episode, not a form.

## Stage 11 — Institutions, power and elections
- **The Musicians' Union** (inspired by bodies like MUSIGA), with an **elected President**.
  - Elections each game year: candidates campaign, debate, make promises and form alliances. Members vote.
  - **Powers within limits:** the union budget (from dues), festival themes, support funds, award categories, minimum feature fees, strike calls.
  - Scandals, impeachment votes, term limits.
- **The Awards Academy:** senior members vote on awards, and lobbying and campaigning are part of the game.
- **Later:** a city council and mayor (zoning for venues, festival permits, nightlife rules).
- AI candidates and voters until real players fill them (Stage 19).
- *Done when:* "who becomes President this year?" is something players talk about.

## Stage 12 — Life and legacy
- Dating, marriage, friendship and family. Kids grow up with traits from both parents.
- Health, ageing, burnout, rehab and comebacks.
- **Retirement → the next generation:** play as your child, starting with some money, fame and connections.
- **Hall of Fame** across generations and family trees ("the third generation of the Mensah dynasty").
- *Done when:* players care what happens after their character retires.

The world follows `abroad || player.country`, so a Nigerian start already begins in a Nigerian-built city. Flights land you at that country's airport door. District toasts show the country's first city. Next: story branching by start country, per-country place names/NPCs/currency, abroad-aware shows & missions, ports/ferries.

### Overhaul slice 11 — story by start country
`story/region.ts` maps your home country (and where you currently are) to a region; the store mirrors it into flags `from:<region>`, `at:<region>`, `away`. New arc `roots` (src/story/content/roots.ts): Mimi's region-flavoured opening call, Big Moses's "a desk at the airport" invite (tied to the flights), and a "you landed" call after you fly. Region-gated lines live inside shared nodes, so any arc can now branch by start country with `{k:'flag',id:'from:europe'}`. Next: fully separate openings per region (own cast, own kitchen), local place names/currency, abroad-aware shows.

### Overhaul slice 12 — local names
`localPlace()` renames non-story places per country (airport, stadium, harbour, radio, bank, mall); the kitchen, Studio 9, label etc. keep their names so the story still reads. Map list uses the local names. Currency/units are still dollars everywhere — a game-wide change for later.

### Fix — big-world freeze
The 900 m world froze phones at start: the walker route table solved every-pair shortest paths (~10 s once the map grew), and the whole city was drawn and collision-tested every frame. Routes are now solved per destination on demand; geometry and instances are split into 120 m chunks so off-screen ones are culled; collisions look up a 24 m grid. Software-GL test: start-up longest task 10.6 s → 0.9 s, frames in 3 s 5 → 23.
